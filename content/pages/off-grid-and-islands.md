Many islands and remote communities in Southeast Asia and the Pacific still rely on diesel generators. Adding solar photovoltaics (PV) and batteries cuts fuel cost and emissions, but the system still has to keep voltage and frequency within limits, and the investment has to fit the budget of the utility or the community. I have worked on this problem from optimisation models to village installations.

## What I have worked on

- **An industrial island grid in Indonesia.** From 2015 to 2018, I was a researcher on a project to replace diesel generation in a 24 megavolt-ampere (MVA) isolated network in the Riau Islands, Indonesia: site visits, load measurements, a control and energy management framework, and a roadmap for PV siting, battery sizing, and phased diesel replacement, tested with hardware in the loop. In the modelled case studies, the diesel-only system could not keep voltage within 0.95 to 1.05 per unit; adding PV alone would lower lifetime cost but cause undervoltage at night; PV with batteries would give the lowest cost and keep voltage within range.
- **Phasing the investment.** Starting with a small investment and reinvesting the diesel savings in more PV and batteries each year could reduce the 25-year life-cycle cost by more than a third, with under 4% of it needed upfront.
- **Sizing and dispatch tools.** Optimisation methods and a software tool for siting and sizing PV, batteries, and diesel generators, and a forecast-based dispatch strategy that could cut operating cost by 5% against common benchmarks. In a case study on Isabela Island in the Galápagos, Ecuador, a PV, battery, and diesel system could reduce diesel consumption by 71% and total cost by 56% compared with diesel generators alone.
- **Village electrification.** In 2017 I co-founded Alva Energi, which has installed communal off-grid PV for villages in Sumba (East Nusa Tenggara) and off-grid PV for an orangutan sanctuary in North Sumatra, among other projects. In Sumba, I helped design the systems, procure the equipment, install them, and train the local operator. With colleagues at the National University of Singapore (NUS), I also co-authored a framework for community microgrids that power income-generating activities as well as households, with a case study on Sumba.
- **Keeping remote energy systems working.** With Professor Dipti Srinivasan, I edited a book (Springer, 2020) in which practitioners describe solutions applied in remote tropical communities, their impacts, and how to keep them running over the long term.

## Where I have applied it

Beyond the projects above, I led the off-grid PV capacity building programme in Eastern Indonesia for New Zealand's NZMATES programme (2022 to 2023), with a training needs assessment, curricula for a mini-grid laboratory and a university PV course, training of six trainers, and a roadmap for a solar training centre. I also worked on the World Bank-funded feasibility study of lagoon floating PV in Tuvalu. [More on my experience](/experience/).

## Questions I help answer

- How fast can an island grid move from diesel to PV and batteries, and with how much upfront investment?
- What battery size keeps voltage and frequency within limits at night and during cloud transients?
- How should a hybrid system be dispatched in real time?
- What makes a village system keep working after the installers leave?

As the share of solar photovoltaics (PV) grows, system operators have to manage voltage, frequency, reserves, and forecast errors in new ways, and regulators have to decide who provides these services and who pays for them. My work connects the engineering to the economics and the rules: what limits PV in a given network, which measures address it, what each one costs, and how the regulatory framework can put them in place.

## What I have worked on

- **What limits PV, and what can be done about it.** A two-part review of how high PV penetration affects voltage, frequency, protection, harmonics, rotor angle stability, and flexibility requirements, with the penetration limits reported in the literature and the tools needed to check them. The second part assesses the solutions (forecasting, voltage and frequency regulation, harmonic compensation, reactive power management, generator planning and dispatch, and adaptive protection), what limits their implementation, and who bears their cost. They are my most cited papers.
- **The cost of integrating PV.** I proposed a levelised cost of PV integration: the capital and operating cost of an integration measure divided by the PV energy generated over its lifetime. In modelled distribution networks with real weather and price data, combined active and reactive power control was the most cost-effective way to mitigate overvoltage, followed closely by reactive power control and curtailment. Battery storage would need to become more than 80% cheaper to be viable for overvoltage mitigation alone.
- **Reactive power from PV inverters.** My PhD at the National University of Singapore (NUS) priced the reactive power that PV inverters can supply to the grid, including the cost of the extra wear on the inverter. Lifetime reduction made up more than half of that cost at low PV penetration, and less at high penetration, when the support is spread over more systems. The work identified the range of incentives that would make the service worthwhile for PV owners, and local control methods that can perform almost as well as centralised optimisation without any communication between inverters.
- **Behind-the-meter storage that operators cannot see.** In simulations on a standard test system, batteries that are netted out with customer load and not dispatched by the operator could increase ramping requirements, make net demand and prices more volatile, and raise system operating cost. Operators should assess this effect in their own networks before such storage becomes widespread.

## Where I have applied it

- **Singapore, 2019 to 2022.** I led the PV grid integration working group of a national solar forecasting programme for the Energy Market Authority (EMA) and Singapore Power (SP). We modelled Singapore's networks, studied how distributed PV and storage affect capacity adequacy, grid stability, reserve requirements, generation mix, and prices, compared integration measures, and presented the findings to EMA and SP.
- **Indonesia, since 2023.** Through the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit (GIZ), I was one of three main authors, with Energynautics and UMBRA, of two studies for the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (MEMR): a comparative study of ancillary services, which benchmarks reference markets including Singapore, and a regulatory framework and business model for battery energy storage systems (BESS), which compares ownership, contracting (hybrid power purchase agreements, capacity, and tolling agreements), procurement, and pricing. I have also worked with GIZ on rooftop PV hosting capacity and quotas, and trained staff of the state electricity company, PT PLN (Persero), on ancillary services and demand response.

## Questions I help answer

- How much PV can this network host before voltage, frequency, or reserves become binding?
- Which measure (curtailment, inverter reactive power, batteries, or network reinforcement) solves a given problem at least cost?
- Which ancillary services should PV and batteries provide, and how should they be procured and paid for?
- How should regulation treat battery storage: who owns it, how it is contracted, and how it is paid?

Floating photovoltaics (floating PV) places solar modules on reservoirs, lakes, ponds, and, increasingly, near-shore and offshore waters. It suits places where land is scarce, expensive, or contested, and water bodies that already have a grid connection, such as hydropower reservoirs. Yet floating PV still typically costs more upfront than ground-mounted PV, and no system has yet reached its expected 25-year life, so project owners and lenders need site-specific evidence before they commit.

## What I have worked on

- **Global market data.** From 2022 to 2026 I maintained the SERIS Global Floating PV Database at the Solar Energy Research Institute of Singapore (SERIS): about 1,700 projects, over 10 GWp, in 66 countries. It records capacity, cost, float supplier, water body type, and coverage ratio, and it underpins the work below.
- **Design, cost, and risks.** With colleagues, I reviewed how floating PV systems are designed, built, and operated. Installed capacity passed 9.6 GWp in 2024, and median capital expenditure fell to USD 1.25/Wp. Levelised cost of electricity (LCOE) generally remains higher than for land-based PV, but water conservation, synergies with hydropower and aquaculture, and less land-use conflict add value. The main open issues are operations and maintenance in harsher conditions, environmental impacts, and regulatory uncertainty for developers.
- **How much could be built on inland water.** Covering 10% of the area of 249,717 inland reservoirs could host up to 22 TWp, enough to meet global electricity consumption and up to 5% of the world's water demand through avoided evaporation. Trackers raised the specific yield of a typical 10° fixed-tilt system by up to 50% within ±40° latitude, while bifacial modules added up to 4.5%.
- **Offshore floating PV.** A global assessment of territorial waters and exclusive economic zones found that up to 95% of the offshore regions analysed fall within conditions suitable for current floating PV technology. In 84% of regions, less than 2% of the available offshore area would meet current electricity demand, and in 53% of regions that area comes at an LCOE below 15 US cents/kWh. Southeast Asia, particularly Indonesia and Malaysia, emerged as an early deployment zone, owing to its irradiance, bathymetry, and competitive LCOE.
- **Floating PV with hydropower.** Of about 70 hydropower plants in Ecuador, 11 had favourable conditions for large-scale floating PV. Floating PV adds generation and reduces evaporation from the reservoir, which helps keep hydropower output up in dry seasons, when irradiance is typically higher.
- **Yield, reliability, and maintenance.** I contribute to the International Energy Agency Photovoltaic Power Systems Programme (IEA PVPS) Task 13, including its 2025 review of floating PV energy yield, reliability, and maintenance.

## Where I have applied it

As the floating PV expert on teams led by Dornier Suntrace and Trama TecnoAmbiental, I worked on pre-feasibility, feasibility, and options studies funded by the Asian Development Bank (ADB) in the Philippines, the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) in Suriname, USAID in Sri Lanka (30 to 50 MWp), and the World Bank in Mexico, Tuvalu (a lagoon site), and Viet Nam (floating PV with hydropower). The work covered technology selection, energy yield assessment, layout and preliminary engineering design, economic viability, grid interconnection and permitting, and implementation and ownership models. ## Questions I help answer

- Which reservoirs, lakes, lagoons, or coastal sites are suitable, and how much capacity could each host?
- How does the cost of floating PV compare with ground-mounted PV at this site, and what drives the difference?
- What changes in design, energy yield, and maintenance when PV sits on water?
- How can floating PV be combined with an existing hydropower plant?
- Under what conditions could offshore floating PV compete in a given market?

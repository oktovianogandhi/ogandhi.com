Floating photovoltaics (floating PV, or FPV) puts solar modules on water: hydropower and irrigation reservoirs, lakes, ponds, and, increasingly, near-shore and offshore sites. It is attractive where land is scarce or expensive, as in much of Southeast Asia, and where a reservoir already has a grid connection, as at a hydropower plant.

## What I have worked on

From 2022 to 2026 I maintained the SERIS Global Floating PV Database at the Solar Energy Research Institute of Singapore, which covers more than 1,700 projects worldwide. Most of my floating PV research draws on it.

- **Design and implementation.** I was first author of *Design and implementation of floating photovoltaics* in Nature Reviews Clean Technology (2026), a review of how floating PV systems are designed, built and costed.
- **Global status and potential.** *Global floating PV status and potential* (Progress in Energy, 2025) sets out where floating PV has been built and how much more could be.
- **Offshore floating PV.** *Global assessment of offshore floating photovoltaics: technical potential, cost-competitiveness, and deployment pathway* (Energy & Environmental Science, 2025) looks at what it would take for floating PV at sea to compete.
- **Floating PV with hydropower.** *Co-location potential of floating PV with hydropower plants: case study in Ecuador* (Solar, 2025) looks at pairing floating PV with existing hydropower plants.
- **Operations and maintenance.** I was a contributing author to the IEA PVPS Task 13 report *Floating Photovoltaic Power Plants: A Review of Energy Yield, Reliability, and Maintenance* (2025).

## Questions this work helps answer

- Which water bodies are suitable, and how much capacity could they host?
- How does the cost of floating PV compare with ground-mounted PV, and what drives the difference?
- What changes in design, yield and maintenance when PV sits on water rather than land?
- Under what conditions could offshore floating PV become competitive?

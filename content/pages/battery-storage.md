Battery energy storage systems (BESS) can shift energy from solar photovoltaics (PV) to the evening, smooth variable output, and provide fast frequency response and reserves. They are also among the most expensive parts of a solar project, and their value depends on the services they provide, who controls their dispatch, and how they are paid. My work covers both sides: the engineering and economics of storage, and the rules and contracts under which it is built.

## What I have worked on

- **Regulation and business models in Indonesia.** Through the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit (GIZ), I was one of three main authors, with Energynautics and UMBRA, of a study for Indonesia's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (MEMR) on the regulatory framework and business models for BESS, paired with solar or wind and stand-alone. Drawing on international case studies, it compares ownership models, procurement, and contracts (hybrid power purchase agreements (PPAs), capacity agreements, and tolling agreements), and how storage can be paid for the energy, capacity, and ancillary services it provides.
- **Prices and tariffs.** For the same study, I prepared the PPA price analysis for PV and wind with battery storage across interest rates, debt tenors, and target returns. I also built a financial model for MEMR to test draft benchmark prices for PV with battery storage and for stand-alone battery storage, reporting the levelised cost of electricity (LCOE), project and equity returns, and debt service coverage under different financing terms.
- **Batteries in the wider power system.** In simulations on a standard test system, behind-the-meter batteries that the system operator cannot see or dispatch could increase ramping requirements, make net demand and prices more volatile, and raise system operating cost. In modelled distribution networks, battery storage would need to become more than 80% cheaper to be worth installing for PV overvoltage mitigation alone.
- **Sizing and dispatch for island and off-grid systems.** Optimisation methods and a software tool for siting and sizing PV, batteries, and diesel generators, and a forecast-based dispatch strategy that could cut operating cost by 5% against common benchmarks. For a 24 megavolt-ampere (MVA) industrial island grid in Indonesia, PV with batteries would give the lowest cost while keeping voltage within limits, which PV alone could not do at night. Reinvesting diesel savings in more PV and batteries each year could reduce the 25-year life-cycle cost by more than a third, with under 4% of it needed upfront.
- **The cost of storage.** In modelled cases of PV-powered cooling in the tropics, thermal storage (chilled water and ice) had a lower levelised cost of storage (LCOS) than batteries, while batteries raised self-sufficiency more. A global sensitivity and uncertainty analysis then examined which assumptions drive LCOS estimates.
- **Feasibility studies.** Pre-feasibility and feasibility studies covering more than 1 GWh of battery storage, together with more than 7 GWp of PV, for development banks and for private developers and investors in Indonesia, Norway, Pakistan, and Singapore.

## Questions I help answer

- What should a battery in this system do (energy shifting, smoothing, frequency response, or reserves), and how large should it be?
- Who should own and dispatch it, and should it be contracted under a hybrid PPA, a capacity agreement, or a tolling agreement?
- What PPA price or tariff makes PV with storage, or stand-alone storage, bankable under given financing terms?
- How much battery capacity does an island or off-grid system need to replace diesel without losing voltage and frequency control?
- How will behind-the-meter batteries change system operation if the operator cannot see them?

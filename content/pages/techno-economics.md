My techno-economic work connects engineering assumptions (energy yield, degradation, grid constraints, and operating strategy) to the metrics used in investment and policy decisions: the levelised cost of electricity (LCOE), the levelised cost of storage (LCOS), power purchase agreement (PPA) prices, the internal rate of return (IRR), and the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR).

## What I have worked on

- **Feasibility studies.** From 2021 to 2026, I worked on pre-feasibility and feasibility studies covering more than 7 GWp of PV and more than 1 GWh of battery storage, for development banks and for private developers and investors in Indonesia, Norway, Pakistan, and Singapore.
- **Prices and tariffs.** For Indonesia's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources, through GIZ, I prepared the PPA price analysis for PV and wind with battery storage across interest rates, debt tenors, and target returns in the published study on the regulatory framework and business model for battery storage.
- **The cost of integrating PV.** I proposed a levelised cost of PV integration, so that curtailment, inverter reactive power, and batteries can be compared on the same basis as the cost of the PV itself. The same reasoning priced the reactive power that PV inverters can provide, including the cost of inverter wear.
- **Lowering the upfront cost of diesel replacement.** For an Indonesian island grid, starting with a small investment and reinvesting the diesel savings in more PV and batteries each year reduced the 25-year life-cycle cost by more than a third, with under 4% of that cost needed upfront. This addresses the main barrier for utilities and island operators: the initial investment.
- **Storage for cooling.** For PV-powered cooling in the tropics, thermal storage (chilled water and ice) had a lower LCOS than batteries, while batteries raised self-sufficiency more. Thermal storage suited applications that shift more energy, such as hotels and cold stores. Off-grid, adding PV to a diesel baseline cut life-cycle cost by 9% to 10%, and adding batteries by 14% to 17%.
- **Global cost maps.** LCOE maps for bifacial and tracking PV worldwide, and for offshore floating PV across territorial waters and exclusive economic zones.
- **Energy policy.** A decomposition of energy intensity in São Paulo state, Brazil (1995 to 2012), separating the effects of economic structure and energy efficiency.

## Questions I help answer

- What tariff or PPA price makes this PV or PV-plus-storage project bankable, and under which financing terms?
- Which configuration gives the lowest LCOE at this site?
- What will grid integration measures add to the cost of PV, and who should bear it?
- How can a utility or island operator replace diesel when capital is the constraint?

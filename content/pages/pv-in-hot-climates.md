Module datasheets are written for standard test conditions that rarely occur in the tropics or the desert. Heat, humidity, dust and the shape of the local solar resource all change how much energy a PV system produces and how fast it degrades. Choosing the right technology for the climate can matter as much as choosing the cheapest one.

## What I have worked on

- **Field performance in arid conditions.** At the Qatar Environment and Energy Research Institute (QEERI), my work covers PV field performance in arid conditions, inverter reliability, and the techno-economics and life-cycle assessment of PV systems.
- **Testing for real climates.** *Real Testing Conditions (RTC) for photovoltaic systems: A climate-responsive framework based on the TruePower™ Alliance* (Solar Energy, 2026) draws on outdoor test beds in four climate zones.
- **Design for different climates.** I was a contributing author to the IEA PVPS Task 13 report *Optimisation of Photovoltaic Systems for Different Climates* (2025).
- **Bifacial modules and trackers.** *Global techno-economic performance of bifacial and tracking photovoltaic systems* (Joule, 2020) and *Monofacial vs bifacial Si-based PV modules: Which one is more cost-effective?* (Solar Energy, 2018) compare technologies across locations worldwide.
- **Soiling.** I co-authored work on estimating soiling losses from camera images and with deep learning (IEEE Transactions on Sustainable Energy, 2021 and 2023).
- **Agrivoltaics.** *Agrivoltaics for arid climates: Developments, technological trends, and global data-driven technoeconomic assessments* (Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2026) is a QEERI co-authored review.

## Questions this work helps answer

- How far does real-world output depart from datasheet ratings in hot climates?
- When are bifacial modules or single-axis trackers worth the extra cost?
- How much energy does dust cost, and how often is cleaning worth it?
